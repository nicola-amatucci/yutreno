# YuTreno: status, decisions and roadmap

This document lets anyone, person or AI assistant, pick up the development of YuTreno without the history of the conversations in which it was built. The rules for working in the repository are in `CLAUDE.md` and, for other agents, `AGENTS.md`, which they read automatically. The technical documentation is in `docs/`: start from `ARCHITECTURE.md` and the design documents in `docs/design/`. This file adds what neither of them says: where the project stands, what was decided and why, and what comes next.

Status as of 9 November 2026. Development paused that day: the short version, with the roadmap from here, is [STATE_OF_THE_ART.md](STATE_OF_THE_ART.md).

## What YuTreno is

A train driving and railway operations simulator for research and teaching, written in TypeScript. It has a deterministic core with a fixed step of 5 ms, a 3D cab in the browser, ETCS level 2 and an automatic block with SCMT, external programs on public protocols (controller, dynamics, onboard, trackside, regulator), several trains with a timetable, networks, open data feeds and a set of checks. The public repository, github.com/nicola-amatucci/yutreno, starts from a single commit of 10 November 2026; the development history, with one commit per change and the reason for it in the message, stays in the author's private repository (yutreno-dev). LibreCorsa is a reference only and is never modified.

## How to work

- Before any commit that touches `src/` or `scripts/`: `npm run typecheck` and `npm run verify` (23 scripts today, about 15 minutes on a two-core machine, two at a time); while working, `npm run verify -- --quick` and `npm run golden -- --quick` take about a minute and a half together. Do not trust a summary saying they pass: run them.
- A change meant to leave the behaviour alone keeps `npm run golden` unchanged: 282 runs, about 20–28 minutes on a two-core machine (234 until phase 10, 252 until phase 11, 276 until the odometry drift). A change of behaviour on purpose updates `golden/fingerprints.json` in the same commit, with the reason in the message.
- `npm run campaign` must keep the determinism fingerprint `a41b59b99b29cfae` (`06d2b24225a458fd` until the train ahead became a service train in phase 9). A change that moves the campaign on purpose updates `docs/CAMPAIGN.md` in the same commit.
- Commits are atomic and in Conventional Commits form. Files are added one by one, never with `git add -A`. Every commit is pushed to `main` once its checks pass (decision 13).
- Documents are in English in `docs/`, with Italian copies in `docs/it/` kept aligned. Every new requirement gets a row in `docs/traceability/matrix.csv` and a tagged test, then `npx tsx scripts/traceability.ts`.
- When the owner is not available, take the option recommended in the plan, state which one you took, and keep going.
- Work in small, verifiable steps. Every new rule gets a check that fails if the rule is switched off.

### Phase 4, step by step

- **4a, network model: done.** A network is a set of lines that meet in stations with the same name (`src/core/network.ts`, `docs/design/network.md`). Each train runs along a corridor compiled from its itinerary (`route`); a corridor on one line is that line itself, so single-line runs are unchanged byte for byte. A node route gives the station of a node to one train at a time.
- **4c, connections: done.** `connections` in the scenario. The departing train waits within the maximum wait, and the outcome goes into the realised timetable.
- **4d, rolling stock circulations: done.** `block` on the services and `min_turnaround_s`. The delay passes from one service to the next in closed form.
- **4e, regulator on the network: done.** New optional protocol fields and the commands `reroute` and `connection`. Reference rules 6 (release a lost connection) and 7 (reroute around a blocked line). The network `valle-lauro-anello` has a loop.
- **Open data of the network: done.** NeTEx and SIRI validate against the official schemas, and the GTFS validator reports 0 errors.
- **4b, nodes and interlockings: done.**
  - The message-based onboard and the simple RBC work on a network. Each train off the main line has its own balise groups and RBC laid along its corridor.
  - The interlocking trackside works on a network. Each line has its own interlocking. A node has junction points, node routes and a consent at the boundary between the two interlockings. One RBC builds each authority along the train's corridor, across the boundary. The interlockings of the network can also run as an external program, with the same run as in process (`NET-10`, `NET-11`, `docs/design/network.md`).
  - Two trains in opposite directions no longer wait for each other at a node: on the line that ends at the node the boundary consent goes with the block consent of the single-track segment next to it, and a route in from a node frees its overlap once the train has stood still for 30 s (`NET-12`).
  - With the interlocking trackside the regulator can reroute a train before it appears: with a regulator the node routes of every movement the tables allow are generated at the start, and the trackside receives the new corridor and timetables as commands (`NET-10`, `NET-11`).
  - The capacity of the node is checked with UIC 406 for two different trains on the routes they share: a train into the branch followed by one straight on (computed 247.1 s, measured 254 s) and a train out of the branch followed by one joining at the node (537.0 s and 534 s) (`CAP3`, `docs/CAPACITY.md`).
  - Left for later: more than one RBC on a network (the Subset-039 handover works on a single line since phase 10, not at a node); one interlocking per station rather than per line; nodes inside a station of both lines; geometry of the junction and flank protection.

### The control room: done

Brought forward from phase 7 right after phase 4. The *Control room* tab shows a schematic of the whole network with signals, occupied sections or block sections, single-track orientation, node routes, and with the interlocking routes and points, with the trains coloured by delay on every line they occupy; a train table, the log and the existing commands (time speed, and the commands of the interlocking). The page only draws: the engine exposes one public, versioned snapshot, `Simulation.controlRoom()`, served also on `/control-room` by the open data server, and the schematic is laid out from the topology of the network, so it also works for imported networks. Checks: `verify-control-room` (snapshot against the engine, determinism, every active train on the schematic) and `scripts/control-room-shots.mjs` (every included scenario opened in the page with Playwright, with and without the interlocking). See `docs/CONTROL_ROOM.md`. Those left for later then were done in phase 7 (7c, 7e, 7f).

### Phase 5, step by step

- **5a, importer: done.** `npm run import-infra` builds a line (`LineDef`) from an Overpass extract of OpenStreetMap and, optionally, a RINF SPARQL result (`src/infra/`, `docs/INFRA_IMPORT.md`). OSM gives the path of the running tracks, stations, stop positions, tunnels, mapped signals and altitudes; where both sources give a speed the RINF one is kept (decision 10; the lower one until 7 November); gradient, protection and electrification come from RINF where present. Every datum has a provenance (source, derived, generated) and the report says how much of the line is real, the licences (ODbL, ERA terms of use) and the consistency of the two sources. The block length is derived from the mapped main signals; the signals of the simulation are still generated. `verify-infra` (`INF1`–`INF6`) checks it on synthetic sources, `examples/infra/`, and runs the imported line with `examples/ato.js`.
- **The first real line is waiting for data.** The development environment cannot reach Overpass, openstreetmap.org, Geofabrik or the ERA endpoints (the egress proxy refuses them). Either the owner allows those domains in the claude.ai settings (Capabilities), or provides the Overpass extract and the RINF result as files with the queries of `docs/INFRA_IMPORT.md`. The RINF query must be checked on the knowledge graph on first use.
- **5b, railML 3.2 and plausibility: done.** `--railml-out` writes the infrastructure of any line in railML 3.2 and `--railml` reads it back, within 5 cm for the imported and the built-in lines (`INF7`). `npm run plausibility` compares the running times of a timetable with those simulated on the line, stretch by stretch, within declared bounds (0.98 and 1.35 × simulated + 120 s, `INF8`).
- **5c, first real line: Avellino – Benevento, done.** Chosen by default (a Campanian single-track line, as the plan suggests). The owner downloaded the Overpass extract; the altitudes come from EU-DEM 25 m through OpenTopoData, fetched with the built-in browser, because OpenStreetMap has none on this line (new `--elevation`, `src/infra/dem.ts`: tunnels and bridges bridged, 400 m smoothing). 30.5 km, ten stations, speeds and SSC/SCMT from OSM; minimum radius 155 m, maximum gradient 21.2 ‰; `examples/ato.js` runs it in 2248 s with automatic block. The plan is now resampled every 25 m, because the uniform Catmull-Rom spline made loops with uneven vertices (curves of 22 m). The line is **not in RINF**; the RINF query was rewritten for the current ERA knowledge graph (`graph.data.era.europa.eu`, `era:nationalLine`, tracks in `era:hasPart`, lengths in km) and checked on Cancello – Benevento. `verify-infra` INF9 and INF10; files in `examples/infra/avellino-benevento/`.
- **5d, a line also in RINF: Cancello – Benevento, done.** The owner downloaded the Overpass extract and ran the RINF query himself. 49.3 km, 12 stations, all matched to RINF (shortened RINF names such as `S.MARIA A VICO` are now matched). OSM and RINF agree within 1 % on 10 of 11 stretches; on the 11th RINF registers 955 km for a 944 m section (metres entered as kilometres), and the report flags it. Speeds 80 km/h from RINF, gradients from EU-DEM (max 30.2 ‰), run in 3406 s with automatic block. Two fixes came from it: the path search no longer reverses through the points of a station (max 45° turn per node, start from any track of the first station), and the DEM profile uses a 1 km running median and a 1 km average, against untagged cuttings. `INF11`; files in `examples/infra/cancello-benevento/`. The EAV GTFS the owner sent has the route «Napoli – Benevento» with no trips, so there is still no real timetable to check plausibility against.
- **5e, the two real lines as a network: done.** `scenarios/services/benevento.json` (written by `scripts/real-network.ts` from `examples/infra`) joins Cancello – Benevento (main line) and Avellino – Benevento at Benevento. The controlled train runs Cancello – Benevento, R 7101 Avellino – Benevento, and R 7102 leaves Benevento for Cancello in connection with R 7101 and crosses the controlled train at Benevento, waiting at the exit signal until it arrives; automatic block, no interventions, deterministic (`NET-13`).
- **No real timetable for these lines.** The owner pointed to the Italian access point (CCISS, `cciss.it/nap/mmtis`, downloads without login). In its NeTEx Trenitalia serves Avellino – Benevento with buses (`busTRENITALIA`) and EAV publishes no trips on Napoli – Benevento (as in its GTFS). Plausibility against a real timetable needs another line.
- **Tutorial and one-command import: done.** `docs/LINE_TUTORIAL.md` (Italian copy `docs/it/TUTORIAL_LINEA.md`) takes a line from scratch, by hand or with `npm run line-from-sources` (`src/server/line-from-sources.ts`), which downloads OpenStreetMap, RINF and EU-DEM, saves every download and resumes from them; the queries are in `src/infra/queries.ts`. `INF12` runs it with the saved sources in place of the network.
- **5f, a real line against its real timetable: Salerno – Mercato San Severino, done.** The owner asked for "a line for which you have timetables". In the NeTEx of the Campania region on the access point (RAP Campania, Trenitalia file) this line has 21 daily rail trips; they are kept as a small extract, `timetable.json`. The owner downloaded the Overpass extract; RINF (line C148) and EU-DEM were fetched with the built-in browser. 18.4 km, nine stations, OSM and RINF within 1.2 % on every stretch, the Fratte – Pellezzano tunnel bridged in the profile. Of the eleven trips towards Mercato San Severino, no stretch was faster than the simulated train except Salerno – Salerno Irno in trip 5018: OpenStreetMap gives 30 km/h in the tunnel there, RINF 60 km/h, the import took the lower and the timetable said RINF is right. With decision 10 RINF is kept and no stretch is faster. 63 of 80 stretches are within the upper bound; the others have margins of several minutes. `INF13`; files in `examples/infra/salerno-mercato-s-severino/`.
- **Scenario configuration: done.** Also asked by the owner: choose the line and the number of trains with a JSON. `scenarioFromConfig` (`src/core/scenario-config.ts`, `docs/SCENARIO_CONFIG.md`) turns `{line, trains: {count, headway_s, directions}}` into a scenario, with services generated from the line timetable or taken from a real timetable extract from a given time; `npm run make-scenario` writes it and `npm run headless -- --scenario` runs the configuration directly. A run whose timetable goes beyond 7200 s now lasts until an hour after its last time (runs within 7200 s are unchanged). `verify-scenario-config`; examples in `examples/scenario-config/`.
- **5g, the two open points of the import: done (7 Nov).** The speed of RINF is kept where RINF covers the line (decision 10), the OSM limits that differ are listed in the report with the source kept, and `--speed-policy lower` gives the old rule. The gradient profile of RINF (`era:gradientProfile`) is read and placed on the line by the kilometres of the stations, with stations of other lines and changes outside the line left out with warnings; the report compares the climb with RINF against the DEM stretch by stretch (Salerno – Mercato San Severino within 4 m, Cancello – Benevento up to 12 m a stretch). `INF3`, `INF13`, `INF14`.

### Phase 6, step by step

Started on 6 November when the owner said "let's go to the next phase"; the plan of the phase (aggregate model, not agents) was followed as written.
- **6a, demand, stops and capacity: done.** `passengers` in a scenario (`src/core/passengers.ts`, `docs/PASSENGERS.md`): origin–destination flows per hour with optional windows, continuous arrivals (default, real amounts, exact) or Poisson arrivals drawn with the seed of the scenario (whole people). At every stop people alight, then board within seats and standing places, sharing the room in proportion to the queues; those who do not fit are left behind for the next train. Dwell = 12 s + max(boarding, alighting) / doorways × 1.5 s × (1 + 0.6 × standing share), declared defaults of the order of the literature (Weston; TCRP Report 165); the doors stay open until the passengers have finished, for the services and the controlled train. Capacity from `seats`, `standing`, `doors` of the train or from its length.
- **6b, mass on board: done.** 75 kg a person (configurable) added to the mass and to the equivalent mass, per vehicle in the multi-mass model; the brake force follows the mass (load-weighing), so the stopping distance stays and the acceleration slows. The external dynamics receives `payload_kg` (new optional field); the JavaScript, Python and MATLAB/Octave examples add it and `dynamics_point.js` still reproduces the internal point model.
- **6c, measures and open data: done.** `Simulation.passengers()`: arrived, boarded, alighted, waiting, left behind, carried past, mean and maximum wait, journey time, delay weighted on the passengers, passenger-km, load factors per train, energy per passenger-km; headless prints them and writes `_passengers.json`. SIRI-ET carries `RecordedDepartureOccupancy` (valid against the SIRI 2.1 schema) and GTFS-Realtime `occupancy_status` and `occupancy_percentage`. The scenario configuration copies `passengers`; example `examples/scenario-config/valle-lauro-passengers.json`.
- `verify-passengers` (`PAX1`–`PAX8`): conservation, dwell in closed form, capacity, mass against the theory (time from 20 to 40 km/h × 1.215 for a mass ratio of 1.217, stopping distance within 2 %), determinism with the seed and no change without demand, external dynamics, open data, the controlled train held by its passengers.
- **6d, passengers who change train: done.** A flow with `via` rides to that station, walks to the other platform (`transfer_s`, 60 s) and takes the first train serving its destination. New measures: changes, transfer wait and its total in passenger-minutes, per station of change, journeys. On the Rocca Sabato branch, 97 passengers changing at San Felice Scalo wait 127 s each with the connection kept and 915 s with it lost (205 against 1474 passenger-minutes) (`PAX10`).
- **Left out, unless decided otherwise:** passengers choosing their route or changing plan when a connection is lost (agent-based passengers, outside the plan).

### Phase 7, step by step

Started on 6 November, late evening, when the owner wrote "Fase 7". The order taken: first what makes the passengers of phase 6 visible, then the tools to set up a run without writing JSON.
- **7a, passengers in the page: done.** A chip in the cab gives, at a stop, who alights and boards and the seconds of boarding left, and while running who is on board; the summary adds passengers, wait, journey, delay and energy per passenger-km. The control room gives the people waiting at every station and on board of every train, through two new optional fields of the snapshot (`waiting`, `passengers`). Reading the passengers no longer changes a run with Poisson arrivals (it drew numbers before): the people arrived since the last call are counted at their expected number (`PAX5`, `PAX9`).
- **7b, a scenario from a form: done.** *Make a scenario* in the *Scenarios* tab: line (in use, built-in, or the real lines of `examples/infra`), number of trains, headway, directions, passengers an hour from each station; for Salerno – Mercato San Severino the trains of its real timetable from a chosen time. It builds the scenario with `scenarioFromConfig`, the same as the command line.
- **7c, a train highlighted in the space-time diagram: done.** The train clicked in the control room stands out in the diagram of the *Timetable* tab.
- `scripts/page-checks.mjs` checks them in Chrome with Playwright and saves screenshots.
- **7d, service campaign: done.** `npm run service-campaign` (`scripts/service-campaign.ts`, `campaigns/services.json`, `docs/CAMPAIGN.md`) sweeps lines, number of trains, headway, directions, signalling and passenger demand, every run a scenario configuration driven by `examples/ato.js`, with punctuality, passenger and energy measures and a fingerprint per run. The example: Valle Lauro and Salerno – Mercato San Severino, 24 runs in about four minutes (`SCF5`).
- **7e, riding in another cab: done.** *Ride in* puts the 3D view in the cab of any train of the run while it is on the main line, running back too (looking towards the origin from the second track in stations); the driver keeps the lever and DMI of the driven train, which is now drawn on the line like the others. The passenger chip follows the train the view is in.
- **7f, the dispatcher: done.** A *Dispatcher* panel in the control room holds a train at its next stop, skips or ends it there, gives it priority, and puts in force or revokes a temporary speed restriction on the main line. The commands go through `Simulation.dispatch()`, the decisions of the regulator protocol plus restrictions, are refused with their reason when they cannot apply, and are recorded with their time in `Simulation.dispatched`, so the same commands repeat the run (`CR2`).
- **7g, people on the platforms: done.** One figure per person waiting, up to 60 a platform, on the platform of the direction they travel in; a pure reading of the passengers.
- **7h, the last two items: done (7 Nov).** *Ride in* now follows a train onto any line of a network: the 3D world is rebuilt from the line its head is on (page check 8). The dispatcher's commands become `dispatch` events of the scenario with *Write into the scenario* (`withDispatched`); they fire where a live command falls, at the end of the step, so the scenario repeats the live run exactly (`CR3`).

### Next

Phases 1 to 7 are done, and the open points of the import (the speed of OSM against RINF, the RINF gradient profile) are closed: the plan born on 5 November is complete.

Phase 8, done on 7 November: a refactoring at unchanged numbers (decision 11). The reason is that `simulation.ts` holds 2,637 lines and 109 methods, 16 % of the code. The steps are:
- **R0, the safety net: done.** `npm run golden` keeps one fingerprint per part (samples, events, timetable, passengers, summaries, dispatcher, control room) of 234 runs: every scenario and configuration in six variants of signalling, onboard, trackside and regulator, and the two campaigns. Two runs in a row agree, and a change of gravity of one part in 100,000 is caught and named. It takes about 11 minutes, not the few minutes hoped for; `--only` runs a subset while working. `npm run verify` runs two checks at a time: 8 minutes instead of 16. The loop of the built-in driver, copied 15 times in eight checks, is `scripts/lib/drive.ts`.
- **R1, `Simulation` as the conductor of the run: done.** In eleven steps, each with the golden master unchanged on all 234 runs and the 22 checks passed, `simulation.ts` went from 2,637 to 745 lines. The work is in modules that take the run as first argument: `trains.ts`, `dynamics.ts`, `onboard-bridge.ts`, `trackside-bridge.ts`, `regulator-bridge.ts`, `commands.ts`, `traffic.ts`, `leader.ts`, `boarding.ts`, `outputs.ts`, `control-room.ts`, with the state types in `run-state.ts` ([ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) has the map). The public methods did not change, and the modules import only types from the class. Some members that were private are now read by the modules and are marked as internal in their comments.
- **R2, faster checks: done.** Profiling the slowest runs showed where the engine spent its time. Three rewrites keep every result byte for byte:
  - the traffic of several trains computes the heads and the spans of the trains once per call instead of once per signal and section, and looks only at the signals of the stations;
  - `toEnglish`, which copies every message of the onboard and the trackside at every cycle, walks it without temporary arrays and closures;
  - the reference onboard keeps the gradient breaks before a target across the forty steps of a bisection.

  The real network went from 20.6 to 12.4 s, a branch with the interlocking from 10.4 to 7.0 s. The 22 checks take 387 s instead of 553 (`verify-network` 258 s instead of 395), and the golden master 512 s instead of about 705. `--quick` runs the short checks (13, 40 s) and the scenarios as written (27 runs, 56 s).
- **R3, the page: done.** First its safety net: `scripts/page-golden.mjs` records the text and fields of every tab and a description of the 3D scene (`YuTreno.scene3d()`) for 24 states, with the clock of the page replaced by a manual one; it catches a sleeper 1 cm wider or a light of another colour in all six scenes. Then `view3d.ts` (959 lines) became five modules without cycles (`view3d-state.ts`, `path3d.ts`, `world3d.ts`, `trains3d.ts`, `cab3d.ts`; `view3d.ts` keeps renderer, lights and the frame, 213 lines), its module variables gathered in one shared state with the TypeScript language service. Then the files of `src/web/`, written with lines up to 473 characters, were formatted with Prettier (`.prettierrc.json`): the bundle is the same once whitespace is removed. The state of the page was already in `state.ts`; the few variables left are each private to its module.
- **R4, the design documents: done**, with each step: the map of the engine in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), the code of each component in its document of `design/`, the tools in [design/tools.md](design/tools.md).

The plan, with the measures, is in the owner's project (`sviluppo/piano-refactoring.md`).

Phase 9, done on 7 November: the train ahead of 0.x is a service train like the others (decision 12). Until then it was a kinematic train that ran at its maximum speed without looking at the signals, with special cases in the block, the traffic, the trackside and the page. Now `leader.ts` turns the `freight` of the line (or the `leader` of the scenario, whose format did not change) into a freight train among `Simulation.trains`, right after the controlled one:
- **the train**: two locomotives of the heavy freight train and as many loaded wagons as fit in `len` (about 1,085 t for 450 m), at most at `vmax`; with one locomotive it crawled at 4 km/h up the 25 ‰ of the Appennino. `train` can give another one;
- **the run**: it stands at `s0` and leaves when the run starts, passes the stations, makes its stop of `wait` seconds in the station within 400 m of `stopAt` or, away from stations, in open line, and ends its run at the last station. It stands rather than runs because with the interlocking its first authority ends 130 m ahead: running at 80 km/h its onboard braked it to a stop at once;
- **its driver**: the built-in one, which plans its stops at 0.25 m/s² instead of 0.45, because the pneumatic brake of a long train builds up slowly: at 0.45 it stopped 25 and 60 m beyond its markers;
- **everything else is that of every train**: block, single-track orientation (priority −1), ETCS onboard and session with the RBC, faults (`leader_stopped` is a breakdown with the full service brake). It carries no passengers and is not in the open data feeds; with the interlocking the engine sends its End of Mission when its run ends. Services in the opposite direction are allowed with it.

The controlled train now follows a slower train, which starts from standstill and stops properly: its arrival delay grew in every scenario with a train ahead (for example 55 → 460 s in the wet descent, 0 → 422 s in the track circuit failure). With the owner's choice, the delay threshold of the five scenarios that would have failed was raised (`leader-stopped-etcs` and `-scmt` 300 → 540 s, `wet-descent-worksite` 120 → 540 s, `worksite-speed-restriction` 240 → 360 s, `trackside/tvps-failure` 300 → 480 s), and that of `radio-loss`, which passed with no margin at 180 s, to 240 s: every verdict is the one of before. The ATO references of the two lines with a train ahead, the times of the trackside scenarios and the campaign changed ([CAMPAIGN.md](CAMPAIGN.md#results): 13–22 % instead of 11–22 %, two interventions instead of three, the same 17.9 % and 1.0 %); the lines and scenarios without a train ahead did not. On the way, the response of a service onboard applied its emergency brake to the controlled train: no run had shown it, and it is fixed.

Phase 10, proposed on 8 November: the RBC/RBC handover (Subset-026 §3.15.1 and §5.15, Subset-039), today missing because the whole network has one RBC. The plan, in the owner's project (`sviluppo/piano-handover.md`), keeps it to the interlocking trackside: an optional list `rbcs` of RBC areas and borders in the trackside configuration, an optional `NID_RBC` on the radio messages, the RBC–RBC dialogue of Subset-039 as JSON messages inside the trackside with one cycle of delay, the reference onboard with two radio terminals and two sessions, scenarios with a border on a line and at a node, with faults of the accepting RBC and of the RBC–RBC link. Without `rbcs` no run changes. The steps are H0 (the clauses as rows of the matrix) to H8 (documents). Started on 8 November with the recommended options (decision 14):
- **H0, the clauses: done (8 Nov).** Subset-026 v4.0.0 §3.15.1, §5.15 and packets 42 and 131, and Subset-039 v4.0.0 chapters 4 and 5, read from the official texts of the ERA: 45 new rows of the matrix, all `missing` with what the phase will do or leave out.

## Known limits worth remembering

- With several trains and the trackside of the engine the authority is recomputed from the position at every step; a marker passed at danger is held as the end of authority only with ETCS, outside the interlocking trackside and the networks. Two trains on the same stretch of line are reported, not stopped.
- With the trackside of the engine the node route of 4a is a simple rule, not an interlocking: no points, no flank protection. With the interlocking trackside the node has real points and routes.
- A reroute is only possible before the train appears; with the interlocking trackside only along movements whose routes exist from the start. The import of timetables matches the stops to one line. The line strip and the space-time diagram show only the main line, the cab the line of the train it rides in; the whole network is in the control room.
- The SCMT is a simplified supervision (one curve, aspects known directly). A real model needs RFI specifications that are not public.
- With the interlocking trackside a service keeps its session with the RBC after its run ends; only the train ahead sends End of Mission. The train ahead starts from standstill, because the trackside gives no authority before the run starts.
- Two checks had silently gone red after a change on 6 November and were only caught when the whole suite was run. Always run the whole suite.
