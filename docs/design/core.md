# Simulation core

## Purpose

`src/core/` is the whole simulation, independent of where it runs. The same `Simulation` class runs in the page, in the Web Worker of a JavaScript controller and in Node for headless runs, campaigns and checks. This document covers the parts of the core that are not a subsystem of their own: line compilation, presets, the run loop, scenarios and verdict, and the compatibility layer. Dynamics, signalling, the reference onboard and the RBCs have their own documents.

## Responsibilities

- Compile a line definition into a sampled representation used by every other part.
- Hold the state of a run and advance it with a fixed step, in a fixed order of operations.
- Apply scenario events at their time or position and keep hidden what the driver cannot see.
- Produce the observation for controllers, the CSV log, the event log, the summary and the verdict.
- Accept inputs written with the Italian names of version 0.x.

It is not responsible for drawing, for transports (sockets, processes) or for the logic of external programs.

## Line compilation (`line.ts`)

`compileLine(def)` turns a `LineDef` (plan vertices, altitude profile, stations, limits, tunnels, block length, train ahead, optional `geo` placement on the map used by the open data feeds) into a `CompiledLine` sampled every `STEP` = 10 m: x/y, heading and curvature from a Catmull-Rom spline through the plan vertices, altitude and gradient from the profile, contiguous speed limits, stations with their timetable, tunnels and the signals generated by `genSignals()` (starting signal 250 m after every station, home signal 400 m before, intermediate signals at the block spacing). Query helpers (`gradeOf`, `altOf`, `limitOf`, `tunnelOf`, `xyOf`, `minGrade`) read the samples. Everything downstream works with chainages `s` in metres from the origin.

## Presets (`presets.ts`)

`LINE_PRESETS` (`valle-lauro`, `appennino`), `TRAIN_PRESETS` (`regional`, `high_speed`, `freight`) with their consists and couplers, `DEFAULT_SCENARIO` and the adhesion values per rail condition. Presets are data: changing them changes the reference values in `verify-ato`.

## The run (`simulation.ts`)

`simulation.ts` holds the class, the order of a step and the small public methods; the state types are in `run-state.ts`, and the work is in the modules listed in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md). What follows describes the run as a whole, wherever its code lives.

The constructor takes the line, the train, the conditions (`Scenario`: signalling, time of day, weather, adhesion, onboard, trackside, periods) and an optional `ScenarioDef`. It compiles the line, builds the consist, creates the train ahead (a freight train among `trains`, right after the controlled one, `leader.ts`) and, depending on the conditions, the state of the external dynamics (`ext`), of the message-based onboard (`ob`) and of the interlocking trackside (`tr`).

The state of a train lives in a `TrainRun`: parameters, `SimState`, multi-mass state (`mm`), co-simulation state (`ext`) and message-based onboard (`ob`). `Simulation.trains` holds the trains of the run in the order in which they advance: the controlled train first, `train` = `trains[0]`, then the service trains. The shortcuts `S`, `TR`, `ME`, `mm`, `ext`, `ob` read the controlled train, for the interface, the scripts and the controllers written against them. The dynamics (`dynamics(run, dt)` and the three models), the effective commands and the onboard and external dynamics exchanges take the `TrainRun` they work on; the public methods default to the controlled train. `step()` keeps the global part (exchanges, time, scenario events, train ahead) and calls `stepTrain(run)` for every train. With service trains (`ScenarioDef.services`) the extra trains are created inactive, appear at their origin (`activate`) when the platform is free, are driven every 0.2 s by `autoDriver` (`auto-driver.ts`), and share the signals: `updateSignals` sets the aspects from all the occupants and the starting signals held by `heldSignals`, and every train receives `authorityFromSignals` from its position. Each train records its realised timetable (`realised`), returned with the planned one by `timetable()`. Without services the single-train path is unchanged (same calls, same order). A service running towards the origin has `dir` −1 and runs on `Lr`, the line mirrored by `mirrorLine`, with its own signals; its `S.s` is in the mirrored chainages and `extent()` gives it back in the chainages of the line. With trains in both directions `updateOrientation()` gives each single-track section (`singleTrack()`, between opposite starting signals) to one direction at a time, `occupantsFor(dir)` counts the trains of the other direction only on single track, and `heldSignals(dir)` keeps red the starting signals into sections not oriented for that direction. The train ahead is a `TrainRun` too, right after the controlled train (`Simulation.leader`), built by `leader.ts` from the `freight` of the line: a double-headed freight train (`freight` set: no passengers, not in the feeds) standing at `s0`, with the stops from `leaderStops()`, an open-line halt marked `halt` when its stop is not near a station, and the built-in driver planning its stops at `LEADER_DRIVER_DECEL`. With the interlocking trackside its ARS timetable passes every station, with the departure from the station where it stops sent as a `timetable` command when it is ready (`leaderTick()`), which also sends its End of Mission when its run ends; it has priority −1. With the interlocking trackside and several trains (`multi`, also set by a `line_blocked` event) the field is stepped once per step after all trains, the trackside is timed by `time`, every service gets its timetable in `TracksideConfig.trains` (in running order, also for trains towards the origin, which make the layout add the reverse routes), the responses are routed to the train by `NID_ENGINE` and each train decodes its own authority (`tma`); a finished controlled train leaves the field. With a message-based onboard every train has its own `ob` (the services carry the reference onboard when the controlled train has an external one); after creating the trains the constructor builds one set of balise groups and one `Rbc` shared by all, numbers the trains with `NID_ENGINE` (`ob.engine`) and the engine feeds every train the balises in its direction of running (`bgRef`), so that the onboards of trains running towards the origin see the groups in reverse.

`step(dt = FIXED_DT)` advances by 5 ms in this order: scenario events; block (aspects, movement authority); in-process onboard and trackside exchanges when due; supervision (internal) or application of the onboard outputs; dynamics (internal, or interpolation towards the last external response); field update; checks of overruns, interventions, vigilance, stops and end of run. `sample()` appends a CSV row every `LOG_DT` = 0.5 s.

External programs are served by whoever drives the loop: before a step it asks `dynamicsDue()`, `onboardDue()`, `tracksideDue()` and exchanges the corresponding request and response. `step()` refuses to advance if a due response is missing, so a wrong loop fails loudly instead of producing wrong data. `runLockstep()` in `controller.ts` is the reference loop.

Outputs: `observe(period, version)` (see [protocols.md](protocols.md)), `csv()` with `CSV_HEADER`, the event log `S.events`, `result()`, `summary()`, `verdict()`, `timetable()` and `controlRoom()`. Since phase 8 they live outside the class, as functions of the run: `outputs.ts` (samples of the CSV, observation with the Subset-130 ATO data, results, verdict, realised timetable, summary, and the display helpers `leverLabel`, `aspectName`, `fmtErr`) and `control-room.ts` (`controlRoomSnapshot`). The methods of `Simulation` call them, so callers see no change. They read the run and change nothing in it, except what the caller asks for: `sample()` appends a row to the CSV, and `observe()` moves the cursor of the events it returns.

## Scenarios and verdict (`scenario.ts`)

A `ScenarioDef` has a name, optional line/train/conditions and a list of events with a trigger (`when: { t, pos }`), an optional duration and, for the faults of a train (`brake_degraded`, `traction_reduced`, `train_stopped`), an optional `train` naming the service concerned. `validateScenario()` returns the list of errors in plain words; `resolveScenario()` merges the scenario with a base configuration. Event types are listed in `FAULT_TYPES`; `ANNOUNCED_BY_DEFAULT` decides which ones reach the cab and the controllers. `applyFault()` in `commands.ts` implements each effect (the same module fires the events in `scenarioTick()`, gives the `dispatch` events in `dispatchTick()` and applies the decisions of the regulator and the commands of the dispatcher): the factors of a train are in its `TrainRun` (`brakeFactor`, `tractionFactor`, `heldUntil`, mirrored in `faults` for the controlled train), a blocked stretch goes into `blocks`, which `occupantsFor` adds to the occupants of both directions and which disturbs the interlocking sections it touches. Events trigger on the time of the run (`time`), which goes on after the controlled train has arrived. `evaluate(result, criteria)` compares a `RunResult` with the criteria (defaults in `DEFAULT_CRITERIA`) and returns the verdict with one check per criterion.

## Compatibility layer (`legacy.ts`)

Until version 0.1 every format used Italian names. `toEnglish()` translates keys (glossary `KEYS_IT_EN`) and values (per-key maps `VALUES_IT_EN`, including arrays of strings) of any input; it is applied where inputs enter the core and is idempotent on English inputs. `toLegacy(x, kind)` produces the Italian format of version 0.x for observations and for the three external protocols, keeping the key order, with `parent>key` scoping where the same English key had two Italian names. `isItalian()` detects a 0.x input. The layer is the only place where Italian names live in the code.

## Invariants

- No `Date`, `Math.random` or I/O in `src/core/`.
- The order of operations in `step()` is part of the behaviour: changing it changes the data.
- `observe()` consumes the events since the previous call; calling it twice in the same step loses events for the second caller.
- A field of the observation, of a request or of a response never changes name once published; new fields are optional.

## Tests

`verify-line` (compilation), `verify-physics` and `verify-dynamics` (dynamics), `verify-scenarios` (validation, merging of limits, verdict, effect of every event type, expected outcomes, determinism), `verify-ato` (reference values and determinism of complete runs), `verify-ato-s130` (observation versions and their equivalence through `toLegacy`/`toEnglish`).

New event types, new presets, new observation fields, new CSV columns: [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#frequent-changes) lists the files to touch for each.
